The kitchen runs Levantine. Cuts of lamb and chicken come off the coals, breads come out of the oven, mezze comes cold from the counter. Order across the table — that's how it's meant to land.

The kitchen

Charcoal first.
Everything else after.

The Levantine grill is older than the city it sits in. Wood-coal, hand-cut meat, breads pulled from a hot stone, mezze laid out cold and bright. Al Arabi runs that kitchen, plainly. The shawarma turns on the spit by the door. The kofta hits the coals to order. Nothing arrives that wasn't just made.

Martineau Place puts us a minute from New Street — between the bus stops, the shops, and the office blocks that get hungry at one. Walk in. Take a seat or take it away. The table runs Levantine — lamb and chicken from the grill, mezze in the middle, bread for whoever reaches.
